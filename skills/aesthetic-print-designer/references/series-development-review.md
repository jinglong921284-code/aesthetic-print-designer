# Series Development and Review

Use this reference when developing a print series from the first premise through review, or when reviewing multiple prints, a day of work, or a mixed project archive.

## 1. Establish series identity before design

Create one identity card per series:

| Field | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Series name | User-confirmed name; do not infer from storage location |
| Series type | Narrative system, occasion capsule, aesthetic world, product program, or standalone print |
| Chapter or occasion | Named chapter such as a zodiac sign, holiday, season, or campaign moment |
| Premise | One sentence describing the emotional and visual proposition |
| Symbolic role | What the hero motif must communicate rather than merely depict |
| Stable anchors | Palette world, mark language, motif grammar, material character, and tone |
| Allowed variation | What may change across directions: hierarchy, scale, density, architecture, or product role |
| Exclusions | Literal, generic, copy-prone, off-tone, or unwanted elements |
| Approval state | Candidate signal, approved direction, or production benchmark |

Respect explicit user classification over file names, folders, dates, visual similarity, or prior inference. Keep different series separate even when they were developed on the same day or use the same tools. If ownership is not confirmed, use `unassigned candidate`; do not force a classification.

## 2. Choose the development logic by series type

### Symbolic narrative system

Use for systems whose chapters carry distinct meanings, such as a multi-chapter seasonal or symbolic narrative.

1. Define the parent system before the chapter: shared visual universe, palette logic, mark language, and chapter-to-chapter distinction.
2. Translate the chapter into an emotional or behavioral premise before selecting literal motifs.
3. Assign the recognizable symbol a hero role and add secondary, connector, texture, and negative-space roles that deepen the meaning.
4. Keep the chapter identifiable without relying on text or a stock emblem.
5. Review both chapter quality and future system continuity. A strong first chapter must leave enough formal room for the remaining chapters.

Neutral example: a `Chapter A` direction belongs to its approved parent narrative, not to another series merely because both contain organic motifs or painterly marks. Judge whether the chapter's approved symbolic premise is legible and whether its grammar leaves formal room for later chapters.

### Occasion capsule

Use for a named holiday, gifting, or campaign moment.

1. Define the occasion emotion and commercial window.
2. Distinguish hero motifs from generic holiday shorthand; retain only the symbols that match the approved tone.
3. Build meaningfully different product roles rather than many minor variants. For example: an expressive hero print, an easy stripe, and a quieter coordinate.
4. Preserve approved motif corrections across revisions. When one motif category is replaced by another, remove residual contours, appendages, texture cues, and semantics from the rejected category.
5. Review recognizability, sophistication, assortment balance, and usefulness beyond a single mockup.

Neutral example: an expressive hero, an easy geometric coordinate, and a quiet linear coordinate may share one occasion premise, but each must earn a distinct composition and product role rather than differ only by scale or colour.

### Aesthetic world series

Use for a self-contained visual world defined by spatial, cultural, material, or atmospheric codes.

1. State the world premise and its cultural, spatial, and material codes without reducing it to a literal theme label.
2. Develop a small number of concept mothers with different spatial logic, such as journey, interior-window, or screen-like composition.
3. Extract motif families and composition routes from each mother; do not treat every crop, scale change, or repeat repair as a new design.
4. Select repeat architecture according to movement: straight repeat for stable fields, half-drop for large focal motifs, directional repeat for bands or screens, and placement/engineered use when the scene should not be flattened into an all-over.
5. Review world coherence and individual product roles. Preserve tactile collage character while removing grids, tracks, repeated focal islands, and overly literal scene repetition.

Neutral example: route-like fields, framed interior views, and screen-like compositions may belong to one approved visual world. A direction from another series does not enter it merely because one motif or colour overlaps; reassignment must be explicit.

## 3. Execute the design loop

For every direction, keep these stages distinct:

1. `Concept mother`: establishes premise, motif grammar, hierarchy, palette, and mark character.
2. `Architecture candidate`: converts the concept into placement, border, engineered, directional, half-drop, or four-way repeat logic.
3. `Repeat candidate`: clean flat artwork with no mockup, model, text, frame, shadow, or perspective.
4. `Initial validation`: edge data, half-offset check, and 3 x 3 preview. Record `edge_lock_failed`, `pending_visual_review`, or the actual visual result.
5. `Bounded repair`: repair only named problems while preserving approved anchors. Use the half-offset central-cross method for seam repair; do not redesign the whole tile unless the premise failed.
6. `Finalized tile`: lock the outer boundary after central repair.
7. `Final validation`: rerun the tool and record human visual evidence. Edge locking alone never proves a successful repeat.
8. `Product preview`: evaluate scale, density, placement, and garment interaction only after the print itself is coherent.
9. `Quality review`: apply hard gates, weighted scoring, and one decision: `Fail`, `Revise`, or `Pass to sample`.

Do not count offset checks, 3 x 3 previews, half-offset repairs, validation JSON files, mockups, or colour-callout images as separate print designs.

## 4. Review a mixed day or archive

Use this order:

1. Resolve the review window and exclusions.
2. Inventory all likely artifacts, including local files, online-canvas-only candidates, validation reports, evaluations, and mockups.
3. Deduplicate by design lineage: concept mother -> candidate -> repair -> final tile -> preview -> review record.
4. Assign each lineage to a confirmed series identity card. Keep unresolved items separate.
5. Count three things separately: `series`, `design directions`, and `technical artifacts`.
6. Review within each series first: premise fit, motif roles, composition, palette, architecture, repeat/placement integrity, product role, and approval state.
7. Compare series only at the portfolio level: distinct purpose, audience, season/occasion, visual overlap, workload, and next gate.
8. Report evidence gaps. Online-only or unexported work must be labelled unverified; missing commercial and production inputs remain `pending`.

Never infer that a directory with the most files represents the only series. Never equate a large artifact count with many finished designs.

## 5. Audit decisions

Separate four verdict layers:

| Layer | Question | Valid outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| Series identity | Does the work belong here? | confirmed, unassigned, reassignment requested |
| Creative review | Does the direction express the premise? | reject, revise, retain candidate, approved direction |
| Digital integrity | Does the repeat or placement file work technically? | failed, pending visual review, digital seamless repeat passed, placement evidence complete |
| Development gate | May it advance? | Fail, Revise, Pass to sample |

`digital seamless repeat passed` is not `Pass to sample`. A technically seamless tile may still fail series fit, originality, composition, scale, product role, palette, or material requirements. A visually approved direction remains a candidate until the user or named approver confirms it. Document entry is not approval.

## 6. Review output

Return:

1. corrected series map;
2. deduplicated direction count per series;
3. strongest direction and its role within each series;
4. rejected or revise directions with no more than three priority changes each;
5. digital validation state and evidence path;
6. creative approval state;
7. open product, physical scale, DPI, colour mode, fabric, process, supplier, and sampling inputs;
8. one next gate per series.

Do not flatten different series into one ranking. First decide whether each series has a coherent internal system; only then decide which series deserves priority in the broader portfolio.
